01The challenge

The International Space Settlement Design Competition runs like an industry proposal shop. A team is assigned, not chosen, an RFP lands, and a complete settlement design and presentation are due in 24 to 48 hours, then defended in front of judges who are former NASA engineers.

02My contribution

Two sections were mine. On Bellevistat, Team Bread's proposal for the 30th ISSDC (2022–23), I designed nearly all of the operations and infrastructure section. On Alaskol, DAFC's lunar settlement (2023), I designed the atmosphere. I also built the team's design database and documented team strategy, which later teams worked from.

03Process & iteration

Bellevistat is an orbital settlement for 7,850 people. I sized its utilities from per-person and per-area assumptions: an Earth-normal air mix at 0.75 atm across 4.29 × 10⁸ m³ of pressurized volume, which works out to roughly 11,400 cargo containers of gas; a power budget of 1.12 × 10⁸ kWh per day, met by 3.37 × 10⁶ m² of solar panels with a reactor as backup; 170 million gallons of water stored in the central shaft; and a day/night cycle copied from Houston in mid-spring. I also wrote the hull material and construction equipment lists, the maintenance and repair plan, and the plan for expanding infrastructure.

Alaskol sits on the Moon next to mining operations. I gave each sector its own atmosphere: residential air at 28% oxygen, industrial and agricultural air close to Earth-normal, carbon dioxide raised to 0.15% in the agricultural unit, and humidity set per sector at 50, 40, and 70%. Residential areas also run a three-season climate calendar. Like every choice in a proposal, the composition had to be defended against the alternatives.

04Outcome & evidence

I led a team to the Asia Regional Finals twice, was invited onto the Chinese national team for the 2022–23 Global Championship, and was named a Global Champion at Kennedy Space Center. After aging out as a competitor, I came back as a student advisor.

05Reflection

You hand off work and trust it comes back, and you decide fast which parts of the problem are worth the time you have left. That carries straight over to engineering.
