01The challenge

A young client needed seating that fit his postural and reach limitations. Our job over eleven weeks was to turn what he and his caregivers needed into something quantified enough to design against: spinal support geometry, ergonomic reach limits, and the requirement that it not look clinical.

02My contribution

I worked on the seating structure design and the fabrication, and shared the requirements work and the fit test with my three teammates.

03Process & iteration

A prototype fit test showed he couldn't reach the push rims. That was a requirement we had written down and gotten wrong. Moving the drive wheels forward fixed the reach and shifted the center of gravity, so we added rear wheels to offset it. One test invalidated an assumption, the fix created a second problem, and we fixed that too, all inside an eleven-week build.

04Outcome & evidence

The finished chair was delivered to the client at the end of the build.

05Reflection

It's the smallest project here and the one that taught me the most about verification. A requirement you haven't tested against the real user is a guess written in a confident font.
