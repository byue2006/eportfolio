01Who it was for

A three-year-old with spinal conditions needed more upper-body support than any wheelchair on the market gave him, and without it he couldn't play with the other kids in his classroom. We had eleven weeks to find out what he and his caregivers actually needed, turn that into requirements we could measure, build to them, and test every one.

The chair had to let him reach the ground to play with toys on the floor, right himself after leaning, move on his own and get over obstacles, keep up with his peers, and be something he actually enjoyed sitting in.

02What was mine

I worked on the seating structure design and the fabrication, and shared requirements elicitation and verification testing with my teammates Anna Karius, Ethan Liao, and Julia Morgan.

03The fit test

We started from a booster highchair with a removable base and built around it: a five-point harness and foam padding for support, side wheels he can push himself, front casters so it turns in place, a cane attachment so caregivers can push him, and a clamp-on tray. He loves Formula 1, so it also got a faux carbon-fiber spoiler and front wing. Not looking like medical equipment ended up being one of our requirements.

A prototype fit test, our first validation against the real user, showed he couldn't reach the push rims. We'd written that requirement down and still gotten it wrong. Moving the drive wheels forward fixed the reach but shifted the center of gravity, so we added rear casters to balance it, which is why the final chair has casters front and back.

04Testing every requirement

We verified the finished chair against every requirement with a target value. All six passed: five directly, and durability by proxy, since two years of service can't be tested in eleven weeks. The chair was delivered to the client at the end of the build.

05Looking back

It's the smallest project here, but it taught me the most about requirements. Until we put him in the chair, ours were really just educated guesses. It also taught me to be upfront about what a test shows: a drop test can stand in for two years of use, but it doesn't prove it, so the table says "proxy" instead of "pass."

The next change would be foam inserts that come out more easily, so the seat can be resized as he grows.
