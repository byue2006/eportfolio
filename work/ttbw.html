01The challenge

A transonic truss-braced wing uses an external strut to carry part of the bending load, which lets the wing be much thinner and longer than a cantilever wing of the same weight. The aerodynamic case is strong. What I wanted to know was what still stands between the configuration and service. A thorough summary of NASA-led work already existed; no review had pulled in the research from outside it.

02My contribution

Sole author. I reviewed and synthesized the literature, wrote the paper, and presented it at the 2026 AIAA AVIATION Forum in San Diego, with travel funded by a departmental grant. Dr. Dowell advised.

03Process & iteration

I worked through 53 sources from NASA, Boeing, and academic and international groups and organized them by discipline: aerodynamics, low-speed aerodynamics, structures and fatigue, aeroelasticity, sizing and MDO, aeroacoustics, icing, simulation, and systems integration. For each, I tracked what has been demonstrated and what is still open.

04Outcome & evidence

Certification. The configuration is novel enough that the usual means of compliance don't transfer. Comparative analysis can't be relied on for icing because there is no operational history to compare against, and current icing simulation tools aren't validated for TTBW certification. On noise, the baseline configuration clears FAA Stage 4 by about 23 EPNdB cumulative, but its sideline margin under Stage 5 is only 2.9 EPNdB, thin enough to be flagged as a certification risk even if modeling bias may explain part of it.

Systems integration. The thin, high-aspect-ratio wing that makes the configuration efficient leaves little room for fuel, so proposals move tanks into the fuselage and struts, at some cost in L/D. Engine placement, the wing-fuselage junction, and control-surface layout get pulled along with it. Each is a decision in one subsystem forcing a change in another.

05Reflection

Certification is the thread I keep coming back to. For something genuinely new, a real part of the engineering is working out how you would prove it at all.
