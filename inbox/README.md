## 3D models (CAD)

Done so far: the shear-force balance (the hero of its page), the forward closure, the standard train head, and the Vanguard shuttle (About page). To add another, upload to the inbox. Claude converts them into a model visitors can rotate and zoom on the project page.

**Onshape** (shear-force balance, forward closure):

1. Open the document. At the bottom, right-click the tab you want: the **Assembly** tab for the whole balance, or the **Part Studio** tab for the closure.
2. Click **Export…**
3. **Format: STEP**. Leave the other options as they are. Choose **Download**, then **Export**.

**SolidWorks:** File → Save As → "Save as type: **STEP AP214 (*.step)**" → Save.

Name the files `closure.step`, `shear-balance.step`, `carriage.step` (or tell Claude which is which). Over 25 MB? Right-click → Compress to ZIP, and upload the zip.

Only include parts you designed, plus the off-the-shelf parts they bolt to (rail, carriage, load cell). Leave out the NC State tunnel hardware, and check with the lab and Duke Aero that they're fine with the model being public.

## "File too large" when uploading?

GitHub's upload page takes files up to **25 MB each**. Full-resolution edits from Lightroom are often bigger. The site never shows a photo larger than 2000 px, so export smaller copies.

**Lightroom Classic:**

1. In the Library grid, select the photos (Ctrl+click, or Ctrl+A for all).
2. Press **Ctrl+Shift+E** (File → Export).
3. **Export Location:** pick a folder, e.g. Desktop → "Portfolio export".
4. **File Settings:** Image Format **JPEG**, Quality **80**, Color Space **sRGB**.
5. **Image Sizing:** tick **Resize to Fit**, choose **Long Edge**, type **3000** pixels.
6. **Metadata:** choose **Copyright Only**. This also removes GPS location.
7. Click **Add** (bottom-left) and name the preset "Portfolio web", so next time it's one click.
8. Click **Export**.

Each photo comes out around 1–3 MB, well under the limit. Upload those exported copies, not the originals.

**Phone or other apps:** export or share as JPG at "Large" or about 3000 px instead of "Actual size".
